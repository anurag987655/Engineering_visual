## What is a field?

A field is a **value everywhere at once**. Temperature is a field — every
point in the room has a temperature. Wind is a field — every point has a
speed and a direction.

The electric field $\vec{E}$ is a field of vectors. Every point in space
carries an arrow that says: *if you placed a positive test charge here, it
would be pushed in this direction, with this much force per unit charge.*

## The mathematics

A single point charge $Q$ creates a field that points straight away from
(positive) or toward (negative) the charge:

$$
\vec{E}(\vec{r}) = k\frac{Q}{r^2}\,\hat{r}
$$

where $r$ is the distance from the charge, $\hat{r}$ is the unit vector
pointing outward from it, and $k$ is Coulomb's constant.

The field falls off as $1/r^2$. Halve the distance and the field
quadruples; double it and the field drops by a factor of four. This is why
arrows closest to a charge are the longest.

With several charges, each charge contributes a field at every point, and
the total field is the **vector sum**:

$$
\vec{E}^{\text{total}} = \sum_i k\frac{Q_i}{r_i^2}\,\hat{r}_i
$$

That is superposition: fields add, charge by charge. It is the entire
physics of the playground — the arrows you see are literally this sum,
evaluated at thousands of sample points.

<KeyIdea>
  The electric field is not a "current" flowing somewhere. It is a snapshot
  of forces waiting to act. Nothing moves until you place a charge and let it
  respond.
</KeyIdea>

## In code

Superposition is just a loop. This snippet computes the field at any point
by summing the pull of every charge:

```ts
function fieldAt(p, charges) {
  let ex = 0;
  let ey = 0;
  for (const { q, x, y } of charges) {
    const dx = p.x - x;
    const dy = p.y - y;
    const r2 = dx * dx + dy * dy;
    const invR = 1 / Math.max(Math.sqrt(r2), 0.2);
    const e = q * invR * invR * invR;
    ex += dx * e;
    ey += dy * e;
  }
  return normalize(ex, ey);
}
```

The playground runs this loop for one charge or ten — the structure never
changes, only the amount of arithmetic.
