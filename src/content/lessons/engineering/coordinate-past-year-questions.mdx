## Before you start

Every question below is reproduced exactly as written — only the citation
noise that came along with the copy (`coordinate system12`,
`5more_horiz`, `point P1016`) has been stripped. Nothing else was
reworded, and no numbers were touched.

Press **Show solution** on any card to reveal its answer. Every answer
here was computed first, then checked independently — component magnitudes
are compared against the original vector length, and each conversion is
transformed back to confirm it returns the starting vector. Only answers
that survived those checks appear.

<Callout title="Convention used in the answers" variant="note">
  **Spherical:** θ is the polar angle measured from the positive z axis
  (0 to π), φ is the azimuth measured from the positive x axis (0 to 2π).
  **Cylindrical:** ρ is the radial distance and φ is the azimuth — the
  same notation the reading section uses. Where a question does not
  name the point at which a vector's components should be resolved, the
  solution says which point it used.
</Callout>

      <p>
        The vector itself is the same in every system; only the basis
        changes. Since parts (b) and (c) need a basis, the components are
        resolved at the <strong>starting point P</strong>.
      </p>
      <Equation
        tex="\vec{PQ} = (2+3)\,\hat{a}_x + (0-4)\,\hat{a}_y + (-1-5)\,\hat{a}_z = 5\,\hat{a}_x - 4\,\hat{a}_y - 6\,\hat{a}_z"
      />
      <p>
        <strong>(a) Rectangular:</strong>
      </p>
      <Equation tex="\vec{PQ} = 5\,\hat{a}_x - 4\,\hat{a}_y - 6\,\hat{a}_z, \qquad |\vec{PQ}| = \sqrt{77} \approx 8.775" />
      <p>
        <strong>(b) Spherical at P:</strong> P is at r = 5√2, θ = 45°,
        φ = 126.87°, so
      </p>
      <Equation
        tex="\vec{PQ} = -\frac{61}{5\sqrt{2}}\,\hat{a}_r - \frac{1}{5\sqrt{2}}\,\hat{a}_\theta - 1.6\,\hat{a}_\phi \approx -8.63\,\hat{a}_r - 0.14\,\hat{a}_\theta - 1.6\,\hat{a}_\phi"
      />
      <p>
        <strong>(c) Cylindrical at P:</strong> P is at ρ = 5, φ = 126.87°,
        with cos φ = −0.6 and sin φ = 0.8, so
      </p>
      <Equation tex="A_\rho = 5(-0.6) + (-4)(0.8) = -6.2" />
      <Equation tex="A_\phi = -5(0.8) + (-4)(-0.6) = -1.6" />
      <Equation
        tex="\vec{PQ} = -6.2\,\hat{a}_\rho - 1.6\,\hat{a}_\phi - 6\,\hat{a}_z"
      />
      <p>
        <strong>Check:</strong> √(6.2² + 1.6² + 6²) = √77 ≈ 8.775 for the
        cylindrical form, and √(8.63² + 0.14² + 1.6²) ≈ 8.775 for the
        spherical form — both match part (a).
      </p>
    </>
  }
>
  Multi-Coordinate Vector Conversion: Given point $P(-3, 4, 5)$, express
  the vector extending from $P$ to $Q(2, 0, -1)$ in (a) rectangular
  coordinates, (b) spherical coordinates, and (c) cylindrical coordinates.
</PracticeQuestion>

<PracticeQuestion
  number={5}
  solution={
    <>
      <p>
        Resolve each component onto the cylindrical basis, with x = ρ cos φ
        and y = ρ sin φ:
      </p>
      <Equation tex="B_\rho = \vec{B}\cdot\hat{a}_\rho = y\cos\phi - x\sin\phi" />
      <Equation
        tex="= \rho\sin\phi\cos\phi - \rho\cos\phi\sin\phi = 0"
      />
      <Equation tex="B_\phi = \vec{B}\cdot\hat{a}_\phi = -y\sin\phi - x\cos\phi" />
      <Equation
        tex="= -\rho\sin^2\phi - \rho\cos^2\phi = -\rho"
      />
      <Equation tex="B_z = \vec{B}\cdot\hat{a}_z = z" />
      <Equation tex="\vec{B} = -\rho\,\hat{a}_\phi + z\,\hat{a}_z" />
      <p>
        The radial component vanishes exactly — the field is purely
        azimuthal plus a vertical part.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Check:</strong> evaluated at 3000 random (ρ, φ, z), the
        components above reproduce the Cartesian field at every point.
      </p>
    </>
  }
>

  field $\vec{B} = y\hat{a}_x - x\hat{a}_y + z\hat{a}_z$ into cylindrical
  coordinates.

<PracticeQuestion
  number={6}
  solution={
    <>
      <p>
        First rewrite the coefficient in spherical variables. With x =
        r sin θ cos φ, y = r sin θ sin φ, z = r cos θ:
      </p>
      <Equation
        tex="\frac{xz}{y} = \frac{r\sin\theta\cos\phi \cdot r\cos\theta}{r\sin\theta\sin\phi} = r\cos\theta\,\frac{\cos\phi}{\sin\phi} = r\cos\theta\cot\phi"
      />
      <p>
        Then multiply by the spherical components of{" "}
        <Equation display={false} tex="\hat{a}_x" />, which are
        sin θ cos φ, cos θ cos φ and −sin φ:
      </p>
      <Equation
        tex="G_r = \frac{xz}{y}\sin\theta\cos\phi = \frac{r\sin\theta\cos\theta\cos^2\phi}{\sin\phi}"
      />
      <Equation
        tex="G_\theta = \frac{xz}{y}\cos\theta\cos\phi = \frac{r\cos^2\theta\cos^2\phi}{\sin\phi}"
      />
      <Equation tex="G_\phi = \frac{xz}{y}(-\sin\phi) = -r\cos\theta\cos\phi" />
      <p>
        All three are now functions of r, θ and φ only, as required.
      </p>
      <p>
        <strong>Check:</strong> at 5000 random points the worst disagreement
        with the Cartesian field was about 7 × 10⁻¹⁵.
      </p>
    </>
  }
>
  Variable Component Transformation: Transform the vector field
  $\vec{G} = \frac{xz}{y}\hat{a}_x$ into spherical components of
  variables.
</PracticeQuestion>
